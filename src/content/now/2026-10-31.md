One thing I've noticed is that there's a pretty big difference between building something because you want to and building something because you think it'll look good on your resume.

Sometimes I just want to build something ridiculous because it sounds fun, but then I remember that I should probably be working on something more useful. The struggle is real.

Hell, even trying to find something good to build is getting harder, as everything I seem to think of already exists. Previously, the barrier was more on the implementation side, but I guess now it's heavily on the idea side, as AI is growing rapidly.

Talking about AI, it's insane how good it is today.

Like, a few years back, I'd just use it for auto-suggestions, which felt revolutionary at the time, as the time it took me to write something was drastically reduced. Then I leaned more into prompting as AI got better and better, and by now, I think AI writes almost all the code, as it has gotten just that good.

This is a really conflicting feeling, as on one hand, I'm happy I'm doing more with less time, but on the other hand, it's crazy how good it is, and I feel like the need for developers is probably slashed by 1/10th or even more due to this.

Looking back, I honestly thought things would be a little different by now. I had this idea that finishing my degree would be the big milestone, after which things would gradually fall into place.

<p class="now-beat">Well, turns out finishing your degree is just another checkpoint, and the actual grind continues.</p>
